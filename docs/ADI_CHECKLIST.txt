ADI'S CHECKLIST: TRAINING YOUR PERSONAL MUSIC MODEL
====================================================

How this works
--------------
You do the human parts (decisions, rating songs, trying the robot). Claude builds the tools
and does the training. After each step, copy the "PASTE THIS TO CLAUDE" box into the Claude
panel in VS Code and send it. Each prompt tells Claude where the plan is, so it works even in a
brand-new chat.

The full technical plan is in docs/THREE_LAYER_CHECKPOINTS.md. You do not need to read it.

AFTER EVERY STEP Claude runs the unit tests and writes a report to reports/ (for example
reports/step03_....md). It shows the test results, how well the algorithm performs, every
number with how it was calculated, and what the numbers do not prove. To make one yourself:
  python3 -m rl.report --step N [--labels data/ratings/adi.csv --user adi]
(The reports/ folder is on branch rl/eval-and-profiles until that PR is merged.)

Be honest in every rating. The model learns YOUR taste. If you rate songs to look cool or to
help the robot, it will learn the wrong thing.

Your total time: about 3 to 4 hours, spread over a few days.

Before you start (5 minutes)
----------------------------
[ ] Open the MakeCU project in VS Code and open the Claude panel.
[ ] Laptop camera works, speakers or headphones work, you are on the internet.
[ ] Quiet room with the light in FRONT of your face (not a window behind you).
[ ] Camera permission is allowed for VS Code (System Settings > Privacy & Security > Camera).

  (d) yes (e) accept. Record these in the doc as agreed, then tell me what Step 3 is.

STEP 3 - Check that the camera agrees with you (about 30 minutes)
----------------------------------------------------------------
Why: the robot will learn from your face. We must first find out whether your face tells the
truth about what you like.

  3a. Ask Claude to build the tool.
  3b. Run the tool Claude gives you. You will hear "3, 2, 1, hold a neutral face". Hold a
      relaxed, non-smiling face for about 8 seconds. Then about 40 song clips play, 10 seconds
      each. After each clip press UP if you liked it or DOWN if you did not. React naturally.
      Do not smile on purpose or hold back a smile.
  3c. Claude prints how often the camera and your thumb agreed.

Done when: you have a result like "camera matched your thumb on 78% of 40 songs".
Passing is 75% or more. If it is lower, that is useful, not a failure: Claude will switch to
using only your thumbs and treat the camera as a minor extra.

PASTE THIS TO CLAUDE (3a):
  Read docs/THREE_LAYER_CHECKPOINTS.md. Build checkpoint P0.3: a tool that plays 40 gym and
  party clips, runs the camera with the neutral-face calibration, and records my thumbs up/down
  after each clip, then prints how often the camera sign matches my thumb with a confusion
  table. Save results to data/ (git-ignored). Tell me the exact command to run.

PASTE THIS TO CLAUDE (3c, after you ran it):
  Step 3 done. Here is the output: [paste what the tool printed]. Does it pass the P0.3 gate?


STEP 4 - Rate 150 songs (about 40 minutes, one sitting if you can)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
This is the most important step. Claude builds a small rating tool. For each song you hear a
30-second clip and answer TWO questions:

  Question 1, QUALITY: "Ignoring my own taste, how well-made or widely appreciated is this
                        song?"  1 (poor) to 5 (excellent)
  Question 2, PREFERENCE: "How much do I personally want to hear this?"  1 to 5

Rules:
  - They are different questions. A song can be great but not for you (high Q1, low Q2), or a
    guilty pleasure (low Q1, high Q2). Those disagreements are exactly what teach the model.
  - Use the whole 1 to 5 range. Do not rate everything 3 or 4.
  - Do not look the song up first. Go with your first honest reaction.
  - If you cannot hear a clip, skip it. Take a break every 30 songs.
  - Songs are a mix of hits and unknown songs on purpose.

Done when: the tool says 150 songs rated. 30 of them are kept hidden as the final exam.
Quick self-check: you should find yourself giving different numbers to Q1 and Q2 fairly
often (at least 1 song in 7).

PASTE THIS TO CLAUDE (4a):
  Read docs/THREE_LAYER_CHECKPOINTS.md. Build checkpoint L1.2: a rating tool with 30-second
  iTunes clips, two questions per song (quality 1-5, preference 1-5), about 150 songs spread
  across moods, genres and popularity, with 30 held out as a final test set. Save to
  data/ratings/ (git-ignored) and let me quit and resume. Give me the command.

PASTE THIS TO CLAUDE (4b, when finished):
  Step 4 done: I rated [N] songs. Check the L1.2 gate: how many differ by 2+ points between
  quality and preference?


STEP 5 - Re-rate 20 songs the next day (about 10 minutes)
---------------------------------------------------------
Why: to check that you are consistent. If you rate the same song differently tomorrow, the
ratings are too noisy to learn from, and we need to know that.

Done when: Claude reports how many of the 20 repeats were within 1 point (need 80% or more).

PASTE THIS TO CLAUDE:
  Step 5: start the L1.2 test-retest tool with 20 songs I already rated, then report the
  agreement. Tell me whether my labels are reliable enough to train on.

Done when: Claude reports the Layer 1 results:

  - Does it understand the difference between quality and preference (it should predict your

STEP 7 - Claude fine-tunes it on you (you mostly wait; 1 to 2 hours)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Claude adds a personal layer on top of the base using your training ratings (not the 30 hidden
ones).

Done when: Claude reports whether the personal model beats (a) the base alone and (b) a model
trained only on your ratings from scratch, using your held-out songs.

PASTE THIS TO CLAUDE:
  Step 7: build and train checkpoints L2.1 and L2.2 on my ratings and report the numbers for
  base-only, from-scratch and fine-tuned on my held-out songs. Do not touch the final 30-song
  test set yet.


STEP 8 - The final exam on paper, then the live session (about 90 minutes in total)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  8a. Claude runs the hidden 30 songs ONCE and reports the scores. No changes afterwards.
  8b. Live session. You listen to about 40 songs in each of 3 modes:
        A) random songs for the mood   B) popular songs   C) your personal model.
      You will NOT be told which mode is running. After each clip press UP or DOWN.
      Do not try to help or hurt any mode. Sit the way you normally would.
      Do it in 2 or 3 short sittings with breaks.

Done when: Claude prints the percentage of songs you liked in each mode.
Passing: the personal model beats "popular" by at least 15 points and "random" by at least 25.
With only 40 songs per mode the margin of error is about 15 points, so a small win is only a
hint. Claude will say so.

PASTE THIS TO CLAUDE (8a):

PASTE THIS TO CLAUDE (8b):

PASTE THIS TO CLAUDE (after the sessions):

IF SOMETHING GOES WRONG
-----------------------
- Camera will not open: System Settings > Privacy & Security > Camera, allow VS Code, then try again.
- The tool says "quota" or "429": Google's free daily limit for lookups was used up. It resets in
  about 11 hours. Songs already looked up are saved and not asked again.
- No sound or "no preview": check internet and volume. Some songs have no preview; the tool skips them.
- You are tired: stop. Every tool saves progress. Say "resume" next time.
- You are unsure what a result means: ask Claude "explain that result in plain words and tell
  me whether it passes."
- Anything weird with your data: your ratings and face never leave your laptop. Only song
  titles and artists go to Apple and Google.
